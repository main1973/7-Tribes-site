Systems are built from relationships.

A system is not only a building, law, platform, or budget. It is the repeatable pattern connecting people, incentives, rules, resources, and decisions. When we see only a problem, we often react. When we see the system, we can identify where participation, collaboration, and local action can matter.

This lesson does not promise a shortcut. It gives you a way to observe how a community works before deciding how to contribute.

Read the system before you enter it.

Ask four questions.

  • Who is affected? Notice people, households, workers, businesses, and organizations.
  • How do decisions move? Identify formal rules, informal relationships, and the places where information travels.
  • What value moves? Look at time, trust, money, skills, referrals, and attention—not only cash.
  • Where can people coordinate? Find existing meeting places, institutions, networks, and trusted connectors.
