How Communities Build

Estimated time: 38 minutes · Open lesson · Private completion requires sign-in

COMMUNITIES ARE SYSTEMS

What could a community build if it knew what—and who—it already had?

A functioning community depends on people, skills, businesses, property, infrastructure, institutions, capital, information, relationships, and organization. Building begins by asking what is needed, what already exists, and what is actually missing.

NEED → MAP → CONNECT → ORGANIZE → BUILD → MEASURE
1 · Define the work

Complaints versus problems

Twenty vacant houses are deteriorating. “Somebody needs to do something” describes dissatisfaction; it does not identify the conditions that need investigation.

Which conditions may be part of the problem?

Investigable statement: Twenty vacant properties need ownership, financing, rehabilitation capability, code compliance, management, and a sustainable use.

2 · Lesson 1 connection

Who has authority?

Ask who owns property, controls zoning, issues permits, applies codes, and holds jurisdiction. Do not assume every project can be solved privately or requires government to initiate it.

COMMUNITY↕PROPERTY OWNER↕CITY / LOCAL GOVERNMENT↕COUNTY / STATE / FEDERAL AUTHORITIES WHERE APPLICABLE

Investigate first.

3 · Before funding

Don’t start with money

Money matters, but map what the project requires: property control, design, management, trades, inspection, legal and accounting support, insurance, materials, equipment, transport, financing, residents or customers, and maintenance.

Capability may already exist while property access, coordination, equipment, capital, or another critical piece is missing.

4 · Core tool

Community Capability Map

WHAT DO WE HAVE?WHAT DO WE NEED?WHAT IS MISSING?

Categories include food/agriculture, housing/construction, transportation/logistics, manufacturing, technology, healthcare, education, finance/accounting, legal services, retail, energy, property, media, arts, organization, and project management. Counts are never invented; any future aggregation must be permissioned.

5 · Diagnose precisely

Skills are not enough

Electricians, carpenters, plumbers, and a project manager can be available while property control and financing are missing. The useful conclusion is not “we need more workers”; it is “construction capability exists, and property control and financing are primary gaps.”

AVAILABLEPARTIALMISSINGUNKNOWN
6 · Lesson 4 connection

Businesses as infrastructure

Businesses can be customers, suppliers, contractors, and partners. A rehabilitation project may need a contractor → accountant → insurer → materials supplier → trucking company → technology provider → equipment rental → property services.

7 · Lesson 5 connection

Ownership of the result

After a project succeeds, ask who controls the asset, receives benefits, carries liabilities, maintains it, can sell it, makes major decisions, and continues the work if organizers leave. Individual, business, nonprofit, cooperative, public, partnership, and other lawful structures may be relevant depending on law and circumstances; this lesson recommends none.

8 · Continuity

Building it is only half the job

Projects need some combination of operating revenue or funding, maintenance, management, governance, insurance, accounting, reserves, workers, customers or users, rules, and measurement. A grocery store also needs inventory, payroll, utilities, equipment maintenance, and supplier relationships.
